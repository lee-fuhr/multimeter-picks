The Crenova MS8233D is a solid runner-up with auto-ranging and a backlit display, while the Mastech MS8268 is our editor’s choice for those needing manual range and higher safety ratings.

A decent multimeter is one of those tools you don’t think about until you really need it, and then it’s indispensable. Whether you’re troubleshooting a dead outlet, testing a car battery, or checking continuity on a DIY project, a reliable meter saves time and prevents guesswork. The good news: you don’t have to spend a fortune to get one that’s accurate enough for everyday tasks. When shopping on a budget, focus on three things: resolution (display count), range type (auto vs. manual), and safety rating. A higher count means finer readings, auto-ranging speeds up work, and CAT ratings tell you how much voltage the meter can handle safely. Skip fancy extras you won’t use, and you’ll find plenty of capable meters for a few dollars more than the cheapest options.
